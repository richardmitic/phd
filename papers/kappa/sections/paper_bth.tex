\subsection*{Research methods}
A perceptual test was used to determine listeners' reactions to carefully constructed audio stimuli. Standard statistical analysis was performed on the raw test results, and one particular \gls{dsp} technique assessed in paper \ref{paper:dafx} (\gls{ph} \cite{gomezMusicSimilaritySystems2008}) was used in an attempt to explain the perceptual results.

\subsection*{Results and conclusions}
The paper concluded that when listeners are presented with voice overlayed on top of music, the best panning position for the voice was not always in the default centre position. The reasons for this were unclear due to a shortage of data, but the paper did demonstrate a real-world use of \gls{sip} extraction (using a modified algorithm trialled in paper \ref{paper:dafx}). Most relevant to the research question \ref{rq:usecase}, it revealed that an objective measurement of panning can show detail that is not easily discernable by listening, such as very slight panning away from the centre.
